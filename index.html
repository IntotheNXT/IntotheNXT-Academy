Three gaps we address

Gaps in how organisations develop leadership and management capability.

Three persistent gaps sit between learning, external expertise and sustained capability inside the organisation.

01

The capability transfer gap

Consulting often delivers the answer without transferring the ability to reproduce the thinking.

Organisations pay for external intelligence, but much of it leaves with the consultant. The answer remains. The capability to repeat, adapt and improve the method often does not.

02

The knowing-doing gap

Learning does not automatically change how work gets done.

People leave training with new ideas and methods, but daily pressures and familiar routines quickly return. Without support at the moment of application, much of the learning fades.

03

The management thinking gap

There is a gap between what we know about how people and organisations work and how many organisations are still managed.

Practices around motivation, budgeting, targets, performance, decision-making and control often reflect outdated assumptions about human behaviour.

From knowledge transfer to capability transfer

Learn it. Apply it. Keep improving it.

We want the method to remain available when the real work starts, not only while the trainer or consultant is in the room.

Our programmes start with learning and practice on real situations. Where the management task lends itself to repeatable application, we add AI-enabled Skills or agents that help managers use the method in their actual work.

The programme teaches the thinking.Participants learn the principles, understand the underlying assumptions and practise the approach on real situations.
AI Skills and agents support application where they add real value.They can help managers apply the approach and guide teams through repeatable processes such as strategy sessions, innovation reviews, planning cycles and customer interviews.
Repeated use builds capability.Experience, usage and outcomes can help improve both the management practice and the supporting tools over time.
What capability transfer looks like in practice

From Future-Back Strategy programme to executable AI Skill.

Participants do not simply receive a framework, slides and exercises. They learn the approach, practise it on their own business and can leave with an executable AI Skill, such as a skill.md file or an equivalent in Microsoft Copilot, that helps them run the process again when the real strategic work starts.

The Future-Back Strategy Skill can:

  • guide the management team through each stage of the strategy process
  • ask the questions required at each stage
  • help identify future customer needs and strategic choices
  • generate workshop structures and templates
  • challenge assumptions and expose gaps in the logic
  • document choices, assumptions and decisions
  • retain the method between sessions
  • produce outputs ready for management or leadership review

The training teaches people how to think. The Skill helps them put that thinking into practice.

Leadership Development

Leadership from the inside out.

Eight connected modules begin with what drives human behaviour, then apply that understanding to AI, dilemmas, the leadership role and culture, before moving into strategy, innovation and critical leadership moments.

01

What Really Drives People: Motivation, Emotion and the Unconscious Brain

Understand the psychological, social and organisational forces that shape energy, ownership, initiative and performance.

02

Leading AI Transformation: Human Behaviour Before Technology

Diagnose and shape the human and organisational side of AI adoption, including autonomy, meaning, belonging, identity, status, power and unwritten rules.

03

Tension to Traction: Leading Through Dilemmas

Distinguish problems to solve from dilemmas to reconcile, and turn tension between legitimate values into progress and value.

04

From Control to Trust: Servant Leadership Under Pressure

Shift from leader-as-controller to leader-as-steward, while retaining direction, standards, accountability and performance under pressure.

05

Culture as a Management System: From Values to Behaviour

Understand how leadership behaviour, management processes and everyday choices create culture, then align them with the culture the strategy requires.

06

Future-Back Leadership: Strategy, Innovation and Human Energy

Connect future customer needs, strategic choices, innovation and human energy so that strategy gives people direction and agency.

07

Leadership for Disruptive Innovation

Apply Clayton Christensen's disruption theory to the leadership challenge of building repeatable new growth inside established organisations.

08

Leadership in Action: Conversations, Decisions and Critical Moments

Translate the programme into concrete behaviour in difficult conversations, decisions, conflicts, crises and other moments where leadership is tested.

Current hypotheses about stakeholder jobs to be done

CHRO / L&D

Functional: build capabilities the strategy requires, create consistency and show that development leads to application.

Emotional/social: feel confident recommending the programme and be seen as contributing to business performance.

CEO / Business leader

Functional: change how managers actually lead, decide and execute.

Emotional/social: know the investment addresses real business issues and builds strength inside the organisation.

Participant / Manager

Functional: handle real management situations better and know what to do when the situation is unfamiliar.

Emotional/social: feel capable in difficult moments and be seen as a credible leader, including when using AI.

Participant's manager

Functional: see improved behaviour and reinforce learning without becoming the trainer.

Emotional/social: believe the time away from the job was worthwhile.

Procurement / Finance

Functional: understand what is being bought, compare alternatives and see credible evidence of use and value.

Emotional/social: reduce uncertainty around an investment whose impact is often hard to measure.
